# Outline options

For each decision, a few short options and a suggested pick. **Picked** marks the choice made
(by the agent standing in for the author, see the example's README).

## Hook

1. The cost of a late note: an animated slide is beautiful and expensive to change, and most notes
   arrive after it is built. **Picked.**
2. A before and after: the same slide as bullets and as an animation.
3. A question: "how long did your last talk take to make?"

Suggested: 1. It states the problem the pipeline solves, with no claim we cannot back (gap G1).

## Thread

1. One sentence of a talk, followed from the source through every stage to its rendered frame.
   **Picked.**
2. One reviewer note, followed from the draft back to the stage it belongs to.
3. This talk itself, shown at each of its own stages.

Suggested: 1 as the thread, with 2 and 3 as its last two appearances. The sentence is the guides'
own running example: "the slowest waits halve once the cache is warm".

## Peaks

1. The stages: the sentence changes form at each checkpoint (outline row, spine line, frame, grey
   box, rendered slide). **Picked.**
2. Backtracking: a note on slide 7 travels back to the script stage, and everything after it is
   regenerated. **Picked.**
3. The close: this talk's own greybox frame becomes this very slide. **Picked.**
4. The engine: one click, one frame, checked automatically.

Suggested: 1, 2, 3. Four is a backup slide.

## Structure

1. Three acts: the problem, the pipeline, using it. **Picked.**
2. Two acts: the idea, then a walkthrough of one talk.
3. Stage by stage, nine short sections.

Suggested: 1. Nine sections is a list, not a story.

## Close

1. Return to the hook: the late note now costs a sentence, because it lands where it belongs.
   **Picked**, said over peak 3.
2. The one sentence, alone on the screen.
3. A call to clone the repository.

Suggested: 1, ending on the one sentence.
